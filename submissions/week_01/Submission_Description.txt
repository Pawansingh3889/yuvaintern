This Week 1 report defines a practical data science problem in the Beauty and Wellness sector: helping consumers and retailers identify whether an official cosmetic recall relates to a specific product variant and manufacturing batch. The project focuses on accurate matching and clear, traceable instructions. It examines how similar product names, missing attributes and complicated lot descriptions can lead to irrelevant suggestions or unresolved decisions.

The report explains the business context, possible human benefit and current industry relevance using official European and UK product safety publications. It also reviews existing recall services and academic research on entity matching, so the proposed contribution is grounded in evidence rather than assuming that another barcode scanner is needed.

A reproducible feasibility study supports the problem definition. The French dataset contains 296 retained cosmetic recall notices. An independent Open Beauty Facts catalogue overlaps 30 product identities, but only 17 have usable names. On eight evaluation products, word-based retrieval returned a linked notice first in six cases, while character-based retrieval did so in five. Both methods confused a 250 ml shampoo with an older 300 ml recall involving a different barcode and batch. These small results identify a concrete problem but do not establish market-wide accuracy.

The report sets out objectives, research questions, testable hypotheses, variables, data limitations and an analysis plan, with validation, bias handling, a six-week roadmap and a STAR summary. A second, exploratory question asks whether dated customer complaints could flag a product before its recall. It proceeds only if enough recalled products have earlier dated reviews; a first probe found little overlap. Future AI comparison and reader evaluation are separated from completed work. The next step is to strengthen reviewed examples before selecting a semantic model. The project does not claim Indian applicability, real-time operation, certified product safety or reduced injuries.
