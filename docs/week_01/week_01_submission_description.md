# Week 1 submission description

This Week 1 report defines a business problem in the Beauty and Wellness sector: detecting genuine safety complaints in online customer reviews early enough for a person to investigate them. Shoppers describe rashes, burning, spoiled or contaminated products in public reviews, but brands mostly read reviews for sentiment and star ratings, so these warnings go unnoticed.

The report backs its claims with specific figures. It uses sources from 2023 to 2026 on online beauty sales, the United States rule to report serious cosmetic adverse events within 15 business days, and European and UK safety statistics. It reconstructs the timeline of a 2025 Canadian shampoo recall that was preceded by 48 odour complaints, and works through a real example of the reading load one recalled product creates for a quality analyst.

A feasibility check on 23.9 million Amazon beauty reviews is reported with 14 charts and 30 tables. About 2.6% of reviews contain a safety-related word, 46.1% of those are five-star reviews, and in 23.2% the word is negated, as in "no irritation". Roughly one in five of a small sample described a real complaint. Official recall and adverse event files from Health Canada and the FDA were also inspected and their limits measured.

Five dated objectives and five research questions are stated with testable hypotheses and decision rules, and the problem is linked to brand sentiment and customer trends. The methodology is set out in eight detailed steps, each with its input, procedure, tools, output and check. It covers data versioning, cleaning rules with measured counts and pseudo-code, planned statistical tests, labelling functions for weak supervision, model settings, grouped cross-validation, and an evaluation illustrated with a worked numerical example. A human annotation strategy for 500 to 1,000 reviews includes stratified sampling, double labelling, agreement targets and ethics.

The plan for future steps gives week-by-week tasks with measurable outputs and decision gates, the immediate next actions, rules for what happens if a step fails, and extensions beyond the internship. Limitations such as missing batch data, class imbalance and false alerts are discussed. No model was trained in Week 1.
